Promises vs. Pace

Are the world's biggest economies keeping their clean energy promises?

David Yu · Emiliano Franco · Justin James · Adam Hamadene

Brazil and Italy produce almost exactly the same amount of economic output, about $2.0 trillion in 2024. Yet 87% of Brazil's electricity comes from renewables, compared with 50% of Italy's. So how much does a country's wealth really tell us about how clean its energy is, and are the countries promising the most actually moving the fastest?

Prototype writeup

We began the cleaning of the energy and GDP datasets to standardize the country codes and years in order to compare records across sources. Previously, we created static charts to explore how the energy mix has changed in the world’s ten largest economies. Our charts showed renewable energy supplied a larger share of electricity in ten countries in 2024 compared to 2015. To add detail and interactivity to comparisons like these we created a line graph that encodes “renewables share of electricity”, “coal share of electricity”, “Low-carbon (renewables + nuclear) share”, and “Fossil fuel share of electricity”. Any of the countries can be selected to be shown on this line graph by an adding/ removing mechanic. Their colors are unique and distinct.

The hardest part will be designing a visual comparison(s) that encodes each of the elements included in our dataset with the goal of allowing readers to judge whether each country is on pace to meet its 2030 target. Countries define clean energy differently so, for example, comparing China’s non-fossil electricity target, which includes nuclear power, with Germany’s renewable electricity target requires clear labels. We plan to label each target with what it measures and use a solid line for historical data and a dashed line to show where the country would land if its recent pace continued. Adding policy decisions to the same timeline poses another challenge because several policies can take effect close together, and a change that follows a policy does not establish that the policy caused it. We plan to let readers filter the policy markers by category and select a marker for details so they can explore the timing while keeping the overall trend in view.
